## Medical Necessity Criteria

Bariatric surgery is considered medically necessary when ALL of the following are met:

1. Body Mass Index (BMI) is greater than or equal to 40, OR BMI is greater than or equal to 35 with at least one significant comorbidity (type 2 diabetes, hypertension, obstructive sleep apnea, or non-alcoholic steatohepatitis).
2. Patient has participated in a physician-supervised weight management program for at least 6 consecutive months within the last 2 years without achieving sustained weight loss goals.
3. Patient has completed a psychological evaluation ruling out uncontrolled psychiatric illness or active substance use disorder that would compromise post-operative adherence.
4. Patient has completed a nutritional consultation and understands post-operative dietary requirements.
5. Patient has no contraindicating medical condition that would make surgical risk unacceptable.

## Documentation Requirements

- BMI calculation with height/weight documented within the last 6 months.
- Documented comorbidities with supporting diagnoses (e.g., HbA1c for diabetes, sleep study for OSA).
- Weight management program records including duration and dates of visits.
- Psychological and nutritional evaluation reports.

## Exclusions

- Cosmetic weight-loss procedures without qualifying BMI/comorbidity criteria are not covered.
