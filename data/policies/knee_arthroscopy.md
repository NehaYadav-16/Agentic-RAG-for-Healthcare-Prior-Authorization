## Medical Necessity Criteria

Knee arthroscopy is considered medically necessary when ALL of the following are met:

1. Patient has knee pain, mechanical symptoms (locking, catching, giving way), or effusion persisting for at least 4 weeks.
2. Imaging (X-ray or MRI) demonstrates a meniscal tear, loose body, or chondral lesion consistent with the clinical presentation.
3. Patient has completed at least 4 weeks of conservative management (physical therapy, NSAIDs, activity modification, or a corticosteroid injection) without adequate relief, UNLESS there is a locked knee or acute traumatic bucket-handle tear requiring urgent intervention.
4. Patient does not have advanced tricompartmental osteoarthritis (Kellgren-Lawrence grade 4) as the primary driver of symptoms, for which arthroscopy is not indicated.

## Documentation Requirements

- MRI or X-ray report confirming the structural abnormality must be submitted.
- Conservative treatment history (type and duration) must be documented, or the urgent/acute exception must be clearly justified.
- Physical exam findings supporting mechanical symptoms must be included.

## Exclusions

- Arthroscopic debridement/lavage for isolated osteoarthritis without mechanical symptoms is not covered (per multiple RCTs showing no benefit over sham surgery).
