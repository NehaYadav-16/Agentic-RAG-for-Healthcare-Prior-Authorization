## Medical Necessity Criteria

Lumbar spinal fusion is considered medically necessary when ALL of the following are met:

1. Patient has imaging-confirmed spinal instability, spondylolisthesis (Grade 2 or higher), or recurrent disc herniation at the same level after a prior discectomy.
2. Patient has failed at least 6 months of conservative treatment including physical therapy, medication management, and at least one epidural steroid injection (unless contraindicated).
3. Symptoms significantly limit function (documented via validated outcome measure such as Oswestry Disability Index).
4. A single-level fusion is being requested unless imaging demonstrates multi-level instability.

## Documentation Requirements

- Imaging report (MRI/CT/flexion-extension X-ray) confirming instability.
- Detailed conservative treatment history with dates and outcomes.
- Functional outcome measure score.
- Surgeon's operative plan specifying fusion level(s).

## Exclusions

- Fusion for discogenic back pain without instability, in the absence of failed conservative care, is not covered.
