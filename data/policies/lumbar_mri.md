## Medical Necessity Criteria

Lumbar spine MRI (without contrast) is considered medically necessary when ONE of the following applies:

1. Patient has low back pain or radiculopathy persisting for at least 6 weeks AND has completed a trial of conservative therapy (physical therapy, NSAIDs, or activity modification) without adequate improvement.
2. Patient presents with "red flag" symptoms: progressive neurological deficit, cauda equina syndrome, suspected spinal infection, suspected malignancy with spinal involvement, or significant trauma with suspected fracture.
3. Patient is being evaluated pre-operatively for a planned lumbar spine surgical procedure.
4. Patient has a history of cancer with new or worsening back pain (concern for metastatic disease).

## Documentation Requirements

- Duration and severity of symptoms must be documented in the clinical note.
- If criterion 1 is used, conservative therapy type, duration (minimum 6 weeks), and outcome must be documented.
- If criterion 2 is used, the specific neurological findings must be documented (e.g., motor weakness grade, bowel/bladder dysfunction, saddle anesthesia).
- Prior imaging reports (X-ray) should be included when available.

## Exclusions

- Routine screening MRI without clinical indication is not covered.
- Repeat MRI within 12 months for the same indication without new clinical findings requires peer-to-peer review.
